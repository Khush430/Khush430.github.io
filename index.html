    § About

    Operational data, made legible.

    I work inside one of the most heavily instrumented retail operations in the UK. A customer fulfilment centre generates an enormous amount of event data every hour — and almost none of it is useful until someone shapes it into a question a manager can answer on a shift.

    That shaping is my job. I pull operational and business data with SQL and Python, build reports and dashboards that track efficiency, delays and the metrics teams are actually measured on, and sit with cross-functional teams to turn what the numbers show into something they can change on the floor.

    I came to it from the operation itself — two years picking, packing and dispatching before I moved into analysis — which is why I care less about a clever model and more about whether the person reading the dashboard knows what to do next.

    Customer Churn Prediction — PowerCo

    A churn model for an energy retailer, built on a year of client and pricing history. The work that mattered was the feature engineering: tenure and days-to-renewal pulled out of raw dates, off-peak/peak/mid-peak price differentials computed across periods, interaction terms between the price variables, and log transforms on the skewed consumption features.

    A Random Forest over that feature set separates churners cleanly, and the importance ranking points at three concrete levers — tenure, price difference and consumption pattern — rather than stopping at a score.

    Airline Booking Predictor

    Whether a British Airways customer completes a booking — and, separately, what the reviews say about why they might not. Four modules, run as one pipeline.

    1. 01 Scrape — Skytrax review pages via requests and BeautifulSoup, rate-limited and de-duplicated to CSV.
    2. 02 Analyse — VADER sentiment scoring and LDA topic modelling over lemmatised review text.
    3. 03 Model — Random Forest on 50,000 booking records across 14 features, predicting booking_complete.
    4. 04 Evaluate — AUC-ROC, 5-fold cross-validation and feature importances, because the target class is heavily imbalanced.
